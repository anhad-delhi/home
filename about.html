About Anhad

Beginnings and endings, life and death, start and stop describe a fundamental cycle of living. They ask us to confront the new and the old, the constant and the transient and the inevitability of change that underpins most moments. Anhad brings together nine musicians and a dancer from very different worlds to explore these themes. In a 40-minute original set, a violist trained in Carnatic music plays alongside two Hindustani-trained vocalists. A jazz drummer plays polyrhythmic tunes with the tabla, while a Western classical Cello ties the instruments together. The guitar and bass weave acoustics and rhythm into the music, almost blurring the distinctions between songs. At the centre of the show, a kathak dancer performs to the entire set, responding to each of the instruments, striking up conversations with the violin, the drum and the vocalists to create a seamless moment of simultaneous beginnings and endings. The music is as much a visual experience as a sonic one. Anhad strives to coalesce familiar textures like blues and rock riffs with Raags and Taal: an experimental blend of time and space, art and artist, performer and audience.
